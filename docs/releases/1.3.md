# XON Shader 1.3 — Galactic skies and living clouds

V1.3 brings a new End backdrop, clouds that change shape and a stronger glow from
lava. The focus is on giving each dimension more character while keeping the
effects within a small rendering budget.

![XON 1.3 black hole and End sky development preview](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Danonymos/XON/v1.3/docs/images/v1.3-previews/black-hole.png)

*Development preview rendered with production GLSL. This is a synthetic GPU view,
not a Minecraft screenshot.*

## A new End sky

Blue-violet galaxies, magenta nebulae, pale star bands and dark dust lanes replace
the simpler backdrop. Fine stars sit alongside rarer, brighter points with subtle
twinkling. Galaxy brightness and star brightness have separate controls.

The black hole has an amber-white accretion disk that bends above and below its
dark shadow. Light paths are calculated offline and baked into a texture, giving
the disk its lensed shape without tracing those paths every frame.

## Clouds with more variety

The Overworld now has three formations: **Cumulus**, **Cloudlets** and **Veils**.
The default **Evolving** mode blends them smoothly over a 15-minute cycle. Choose
a fixed formation if you prefer; wind and internal cloud detail keep moving.
The animation also stays continuous across the hourly timer reset.

## More luminous lava

Lava pools and flowing lava feed more energy into bloom, making the hot areas
read more clearly as a light source. Dark crust and orange detail remain visible.
The new **Lava Glow** control adjusts this effect without raising bloom on other
light sources. It requires **Bloom** to be enabled.

## End sky fixes included

- Corrected background blending to remove the washed-out upper sky and hard
  brightness split seen during V1.3 testing.
- Restored texture transparency and fading on End flashes so they no longer
  appear as bright rectangular patches of galaxy.

Both corrections are part of V1.3. The public download is **XON-Shader-v1.3.zip**.

## Settings and installation

1. Put **XON-Shader-v1.3.zip** in your instance's `shaderpacks` folder. Leave it zipped.
2. Select it in Iris and click **Apply**. Start with **Balanced + Native**.
3. Open **Sky & Atmosphere → Cloud Formations / End Sky** for the new sky controls.
   Find **Lava Glow** under **Colored Block Light**.

The existing End Aurora preference now appears as **Galaxy Brightness**. Existing
option identifiers are retained. **Speed** omits the galaxy and black hole and
keeps a quieter star field.

This release also includes V1.2's cleaner settings, richer colored block light,
amber jack-o-lantern auras and cyan-teal soul-lantern auras.

## Performance and checks

- No additional render pass or temporal history buffer.
- Cloud formations share the existing heightfield sampling and lighting probes;
  lava glow uses the existing bloom passes.
- The optional black-hole texture adds about **4 MiB** of texels before driver
  overhead/mipmaps. The galaxy and star calculations add GPU work.
- **1092 OpenGL compile/link configurations** pass with zero shader warnings.
  **947 cumulative GPU cases/comparisons**, client mapping and property checks
  cover the shader paths, including the new End draw-state regressions.

These are controlled tests on an RTX 5060 Ti, not a full-game FPS benchmark.
Live appearance and FPS confirmation of the corrected pack remain outstanding.
The black hole uses a fixed thin-disk view and artistic emissivity; clouds remain
heightfields rather than ray-marched volumes.
